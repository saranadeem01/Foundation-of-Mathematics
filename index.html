Piecewise-defined functions

One function, different formulas on different parts of its domain. Model: |x| = x (x≥0); −x (x<0). Book Example 4:
f(x) = −x (x<0) · x² (0≤x≤1) · 1 (x>1)
Teacher: have students verify that ⌊2.4⌋=2, ⌊−1.2⌋=−2, ⌈−0.3⌉=0 by moving the slider. Emphasise "one x, one output": the whole thing is still a single function with domain (−∞,∞).

Sums, differences, products, quotients

For x in D(f) ∩ D(g): (f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x), likewise f−g and fg. For f/g we also need g(x)≠0. Also (cf)(x)=c·f(x).
Book Example 1: f(x)=√x, g(x)=√(1−x). D(f)=[0,∞), D(g)=(−∞,1], overlap [0,1].

Composite functions

(f∘g)(x) = f(g(x)): do g first, then f. Domain: x in D(g) such that g(x) is in D(f). In general f∘g ≠ g∘f.

Shifting a graph

Vertical: y=f(x)+k: up k units if k>0, down |k| if k<0.
Horizontal: y=f(x+h): left h units if h>0, right |h| if h<0. (Inside the bracket the direction is opposite to the sign!)
Book Example 3: (a) x²+1 up 1, (b) x²−2 down 2, (c) (x+3)² left 3 and (x−2)² right 2, (d) |x−2|−1 right 2 and down 1. Use the preset buttons. Exercises: §1.2 #21–24.
44–54 minThomas §1.2, pp.16–18

Scaling and reflecting a graph

For a factor c>1: cf(x) stretches vertically by c; (1/c)f(x) compresses vertically; f(cx) compresses horizontally by c; f(x/c) stretches horizontally.
Reflect (c = −1): −f(x) across the x-axis; f(−x) across the y-axis.
Book Example 5: f(x)=x⁴−4x³+10.
(a) compress horizontally by 2 and reflect in the y-axis: f(−2x) = 16x⁴ + 32x³ + 10.
(b) compress vertically by 2 and reflect in the x-axis: −½f(x) = −½x⁴ + 2x³ − 5.
Presets reproduce Example 4 on √x (Figs 1.32–1.34). Note √(3x)=√3·√x: a horizontal compression can match a vertical stretch. Exercises: §1.2 #55, 57–66.

Radians and the six trig functions

Radian measure: θ = s/r, so s = rθ; on the unit circle θ is the arc length. π rad = 180°. A point P(x,y) on a circle of radius r at angle θ gives sin=y/r, cos=x/r, tan=y/x, csc=r/y, sec=r/x, cot=x/y.
Periodicity (book): f(x+p)=f(x). tan, cot: period π. sin, cos, sec, csc: period 2π. cos and sec are even; the other four are odd.
CAST rule (Calculus Activates Student Thinking): All positive in I, Sine in II, Tan in III, Cosine in IV. Example: sin 2π/3=√3/2, cos 2π/3=−1/2, tan 2π/3=−√3.

Identities and the general sine curve

Pythagorean identity (Eq. 3), from x²+y²=1:
cos²θ + sin²θ = 1
÷cos²θ: 1+tan²θ = sec²θ
÷sin²θ: 1+cot²θ = csc²θ
Book's toolkit: sin(A+B)=sinA cosB+cosA sinB
cos(A+B)=cosA cosB−sinA sinB
Double angle: sin2θ=2sinθcosθ, cos2θ=cos²θ−sin²θ. Everything in the book derives from Eq. 3 and 4.
General sinusoid (apply §1.2 rules to sin): f(x) = A sin( (2π/B)(x − C) ) + D. |A| = amplitude, |B| = period, C = horizontal shift, D = vertical shift (midline y=D).
